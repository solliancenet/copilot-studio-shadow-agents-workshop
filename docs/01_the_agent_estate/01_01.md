# Task 01-01 — The three questions

## Introduction

Every agent-governance conversation collapses into three questions: **How many agents do we have? Who
owns them? What can they access?** They sound like a five-minute lookup. They are not — and feeling *why*
they are hard, before you have any tooling, is the point of this task. Since May 2026 every new Copilot
Studio agent gets an Entra Agent ID automatically (older agents may still use a legacy app registration),
and Agent 365 (GA May 1, 2026) put an admin surface on top; but a governance surface is not the same as an
answer. You still have to assemble the answer from
data that is scattered across environments, connections, and identities.

## Description

You will take the three questions to the live tenant and try to answer each one for the Halcourt Mutual
estate. This is your first evidence-gathering pass: as you go, you fill in three of your blank CSVs from
the portals and APIs — `agents-export.csv`, `users-export.csv`, and `connections-export.csv` — and those
CSVs feed the inventory and findings you build in Exercise 02. If you can't reach the tenant, work from the
filled-in [artifact pack]({% link worksheets/index.md %}#the-artifact-pack) instead. Along the way you will
notice that no single source answers any question cleanly: the findings live in the *joins* between them.
Record how far you get and where you get stuck.

## Success Criteria

- [ ] *(Live tenant only)* You have filled in your `agents-export.csv`, `users-export.csv`, and
      `connections-export.csv` from the tenant.
- [ ] You can say which columns each of the five CSVs carries.
- [ ] You have a first count of agents, and you can name at least one reason that count might be
      incomplete for a real tenant.
- [ ] You have cross-checked agent and connection owners against account status and account type, and
      recorded anything that weakens clear human accountability.
- [ ] You have attempted "what can they access" for a single agent and listed which sources you had to
      join to get even a partial answer.
- [ ] You can state, in one sentence, why these three questions are hard to answer at a glance.

## Key Tasks

### 01: How many agents do we have?

Get a first count from the admin center, then stress-test whether that count is really complete.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. In the **Power Platform admin center**, choose **Manage** → **Inventory** and filter the list to
   Copilot Studio agents. The tenant may hold other estates' agents: keep the ones in your
   `-<estate_id>` environments, and in `Halcourt (default)` only those whose names carry your estate id.
   Count them. That is your starting number of agents.
2. Choose **Download** to export the list as CSV, and copy your agents into your blank
   [`agents-export.csv`]({% link worksheets/artifact-pack/blank/agents-export.csv %}), one row per agent:
   its name, environment, owner, and created date go in `display_name`, `environment`, `owner_upn`, and
   `created_utc`. The inventory's **Modified on** doesn't work for agents (it shows a dash), so leave
   `last_modified_utc` for the next step.
3. In the **Microsoft 365 admin center** at `https://admin.microsoft.com`, go to **Agents** →
   **All agents** → **Registry** and open each of your agents. Its **Details** tab shows its description,
   status, publish status, and when it was last updated: record them in `description`, `status`,
   `published`, and `last_modified_utc`. Where an agent has no description, write a one-line one of your
   own.

   Without the tenant: count the rows in the filled-in
   [`agents-export.csv`]({% link worksheets/artifact-pack/agents-export.csv %}).
4. Look at the `environment` column. Note how many *distinct* environments the agents live in, then
   compare against your `environments-export.csv` from
   [Task 00-02]({% link docs/00_lab_environment/00_02.md %}). Is every environment represented? Which
   environment types (Default, Production, Sandbox, Developer) are in scope?
5. Ask the harder question: in a real tenant, would this list contain *every* agent? Consider what any
   single inventory can miss: environments or sources you don't have permission to see, agent types that
   surface doesn't cover, agents too new to appear yet, and the same agent listed twice under different
   publishers. Even a complete list carries none of the ownership, credential, grounding, or reach facts
   you need — and an agent in someone's personal environment can be listed and still reviewed by no one.
   Write down which of these would worry you most in a real tenant.
6. Record your count and a one-line caveat about what it might be missing.

</details>

### 02: Who owns them?

An owner column looks like an answer. Cross-check it against the account data and watch it get slippery.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. In your `agents-export.csv`, read the `owner_upn` column for each agent. On its face, every agent has
   an owner.
2. Now look up each owner's account. In the **Microsoft Entra admin center** at
   `https://entra.microsoft.com`, go to **Entra ID** → **Users** and open the owner: the **Overview**
   shows whether the account is enabled and when it last signed in. To check every account at once, run
   this in [Graph Explorer](https://developer.microsoft.com/graph/graph-explorer){:target="_blank"}:

   ```http
   GET https://graph.microsoft.com/v1.0/users?$select=userPrincipalName,displayName,accountEnabled,signInActivity
   ```

   Then, in the **Exchange admin center** at `https://admin.exchange.microsoft.com`, go to **Recipients**
   → **Mailboxes** and filter to shared mailboxes. Record each owner in your blank
   [`users-export.csv`]({% link worksheets/artifact-pack/blank/users-export.csv %}): `upn`,
   `display_name`, `account_enabled`, `last_sign_in_utc`, and `is_shared_mailbox`. `is_service_account`
   is your call — no tenant field marks it, so judge from the account's name and what it is used for.

   Without the tenant: join each `owner_upn` to the filled-in
   [`users-export.csv`]({% link worksheets/artifact-pack/users-export.csv %}) and look at three columns:
   `account_enabled`, `is_service_account`, and `is_shared_mailbox`.
3. Flag any owner that is not a real, present, accountable person — a **disabled** account, a **service
   account**, or a **shared mailbox**. For each, ask: if this agent misbehaved tomorrow, who would you
   call?
4. Now find who owns the *connections* behind your agents. No portal page lists every connection in an
   environment, so ask the Power Platform admin API with the Azure CLI. Sign in with your lab account:

   ```bash
   az login --allow-no-subscriptions
   ```

   List the environments. Each entry's `name` is the environment id, and `properties.displayName` its
   name:

   ```bash
   az rest --method get --url "https://api.bap.microsoft.com/providers/Microsoft.BusinessAppPlatform/scopes/admin/environments?api-version=2021-04-01" --resource https://service.powerapps.com/
   ```

   Then list the connections in each of your environments:

   ```bash
   az rest --method get --url "https://api.powerapps.com/providers/Microsoft.PowerApps/scopes/admin/environments/<environment-id>/connections?api-version=2016-11-01" --resource https://service.powerapps.com/
   ```

   Record each connection in your blank
   [`connections-export.csv`]({% link worksheets/artifact-pack/blank/connections-export.csv %}):
   `properties.displayName` in `connection_name`, the last segment of `properties.apiId` in `connector`,
   the environment's name in `environment`, and `properties.createdBy.userPrincipalName` in
   `connection_owner_upn`. In `Halcourt (default)`, keep only the connections your estate's accounts own.
5. See who each connection is shared with. Here `<connection-name>` is the connection's `name` from the
   call above, not its display name:

   ```bash
   az rest --method get --url "https://api.powerapps.com/providers/Microsoft.PowerApps/scopes/admin/environments/<environment-id>/apis/<connector>/connections/<connection-name>/roleAssignments?api-version=2016-11-01" --resource https://service.powerapps.com/
   ```

   Put everyone other than the owner in `shared_with`.
6. Match the connections to your agents, and find the credentials that aren't connections. In
   **Copilot Studio** at `https://copilotstudio.microsoft.com`, pick each agent's environment in the
   environment switcher and open **Agents** → the agent.
   - Your lab account can **view** agents, not edit them, so the agent's header shows only **Evaluation**
     and **Monitor**, and **Settings** is unavailable. Open its other pages by address: in the browser's
     address bar, replace the `/overview` at the end with `/actions` (the **Tools** page), `/knowledge`
     or `/topics`.
   - On the **Tools** page, select each tool. Its **Details** name what it calls: a connector tool runs
     on one of the connections you listed, and a **Model Context Protocol** tool runs on a connection to
     a custom connector, also in your list. Put the agent's name in that connection's `used_by_agent`. A
     viewer sees the tool's **Connection** as *Not connected*: you can't see another person's connection,
     so that field says nothing about the agent — the connections call has each connection's real status.
   - On the **Knowledge** page, list each source the agent grounds on, such as a SharePoint site or a
     OneDrive folder. An agent with no tools can still read through a connection here. Find the connection
     in your list that reaches that source: the same connector, in the agent's environment, owned by the
     person whose access the source is read with. Put the agent's name in its `used_by_agent`. A viewer
     can't see which connection a knowledge source is bound to; if more than one connection could fit, use
     the `used_by_agent` in the filled-in `connections-export.csv`, and note that the pack is your source.
   - The agent's HTTP requests live inside its topics, on the **Topics** page; the **Tools** page doesn't
     show them. Add a row for each, with a name of your own in `connection_name`, the kind of call in
     `connector`, and the agent's owner in `connection_owner_upn`, since no connection holds its
     credential.
   - If the agent calls a cloud flow, find the flow in the Power Platform admin center under **Manage** →
     **Environments** → the environment → **Resources** → **Flows**. The flow's **Details** show its
     connections and owners. Add a row for the flow, with its owner in `connection_owner_upn`.
7. Write each row's `auth_type` yourself: a short label for whose credential the call carries. For a tool,
   open it from the agent's **Tools** page and, under **Details** → **Additional details**, read whether it
   uses **End user** or **Maker-provided** credentials. Put that together with who owns the
   connection. For an HTTP request, see how the topic's node authenticates (read the node's headers); for
   a flow, look at its connections and owners.

   Without the tenant: use the filled-in
   [`connections-export.csv`]({% link worksheets/artifact-pack/connections-export.csv %}).
8. Compare `connection_owner_upn` against the agent's `owner_upn`. Note any agent where the person who
   owns the *connection* is not the person who owns the *agent*. The connection owner's credential is what
   a **Maker-provided** tool carries; Exercise 03 works out which tools are Maker-provided. If you're
   working in the tenant, add each connection owner who isn't in your `users-export.csv` yet.
9. Hold these observations. You will not resolve them here — Exercise 02 turns them into named findings — but
   noticing that "owner" has several different meanings is the whole point.

</details>

### 03: What can they access?

This is the question the day is really about. Try to answer it for one agent and watch how many sources
you have to touch.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. Pick a single agent from your `agents-export.csv`.
2. Try to write one paragraph describing everything it can reach. To do it, you will need to join across
   sources: your `connections-export.csv` (which connector and auth type), the agent's Entra identity, and
   your `environments-export.csv` (whether the environment has any DLP). Find the identity in the
   **Microsoft Entra admin center** under **Entra ID** → **Agents** → **Agent identities**; you record
   identities in the next task.

   Without the tenant: the identity is in the filled-in
   [`agent-identities-export.csv`]({% link worksheets/artifact-pack/agent-identities-export.csv %}).
3. Notice what is *still* missing even after the joins — for example, the specific SharePoint site or
   Dataverse tables the connection can read, or the exact API the HTTP action calls. That detail lives in
   the agent's configuration and grounding (in Copilot Studio, its **Knowledge** and **Topics** pages), not
   in the CSVs; it is what Exercises 02 and 03 go get.
4. Write down the shortest honest answer you can give right now, and underline the parts you had to leave
   as "unknown — needs the agent's configuration."

</details>

## Summary

You just experienced the gap the rest of the workshop closes. Three plain questions — *how many, who owns
them, what can they access* — have no one-place answer, because an agent's real exposure is spread across
its environment, its connections, its identity, and its grounding. You also met the recurring subtlety in
"who owns them": an owner can be a disabled account, a service account, a shared mailbox, or someone
different from whoever owns the underlying connection. Keep your rough answers and your list of "unknowns"
— Exercise 02 (DISCOVER) turns the first two questions into a real inventory, and Exercise 03 (MAP) turns the
third into effective-access findings.
