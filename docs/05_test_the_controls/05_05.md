# Task 05-05 — Agent 365 governance

## Introduction

Governance tooling — **Agent 365** — is where you get an inventory of every agent and its identity,
ownership records, risk signals, and lifecycle actions such as block. It is the control most likely to *find*
the estate's lifecycle failures for you. The discipline this task teaches is the difference between a control
that **solves** a problem and one that merely **exposes** it: an inventory that lists an orphaned agent has
done something valuable, but the agent is still orphaned until someone acts.

## Description

You will look at the estate the way the Agent 365 registry sees it, record what it detects and what it
doesn't, write the ownership policy the findings call for, then retest **Scenario F** (lifecycle) after
your facilitator blocks Onboarding Guide. For each finding you record whether governance **closed** it
(the agent actually stopped) or only **exposed** it (it appears in a list, and remediation is a separate
action).

## Success Criteria

- [ ] You searched the registry for your estate id, and found the estate's other agents in the list under
  their plain names.
- [ ] You recorded what the **Agents without owners** and **Agents at risk** tiles show, and where the
  registry does show Onboarding Guide's departed owner.
- [ ] You wrote an ownership policy: the owner rule, and one action for each lifecycle finding.
- [ ] You retested Scenario F by reading Onboarding Guide's registry **Status** after your facilitator
  blocked it.
- [ ] You recorded the **Agent 365** column's **F** cell in the
  [Control Efficacy Matrix]({% link worksheets/templates/04-control-efficacy-matrix.md %}), with a
  **Retest log** block.

## Key Tasks

### 01: Find the estate in the registry

The registry is Agent 365's inventory: it lists every Copilot Studio agent in the tenant, not just your
estate's.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. In the Microsoft 365 admin center, select **Agents** → **All agents**, and stay on the **Registry** tab.
2. Read the four summary tiles: **Total agents**, **Agents at risk**, **Agents without owners** and
   **Connected platforms**. They count the whole tenant, not just your estate.
3. Search the list for your estate id.
4. Compare the result with your inventory. Then search for one of your estate's other agents by name (for
   example, Claims Copilot) and note how many results come back.

</details>

Without the tenant: in `agents-export.csv`, note which agents are in the default environment. Those are the
ones whose registry names carry your estate id.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Reveal what the search returns</summary>

**Showing 2 of 2**: **Expense Helper** (2 active users) and **Onboarding Guide** (0). That's expected:
only the default-environment agents carry the estate id in their names. Your estate's other agents
(Claims Copilot, Underwriting Assistant, Sales Nudge and the rest) are in the list under their plain names,
one entry per estate sharing the tenant. The registry lists the whole estate.

</details>

### 02: Check for the orphan and the risk signals

Onboarding Guide's owner, Tom Dean, has left and his account is still enabled; you have that from your
inventory. This checks whether the registry knows it.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. Read the **Agents without owners** tile. If it reads 0 there's no list to open: Onboarding Guide isn't
   counted.
2. Read the **Agents at risk** tile.
3. Select **Onboarding Guide** in the list. Open **Data & tools** and read **Tools**, **Can read** and
   **Knowledge**. Then open the **Details** tab and read **Owner**, **Created by** and **Last used**.
4. Record the result of each check as what the registry does or doesn't detect.

</details>

Without the tenant: record the tiles as unknown, and Onboarding Guide's Outlook connection from
`connections-export.csv`.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Reveal what the checks show</summary>

**Agents without owners** reads 0 and **Agents at risk** reads "—". The registry counts an agent as
ownerless only when its owner's account has been *deleted*. Tom Dean's account still exists, so Onboarding
Guide doesn't count. Under **Data & tools**, **Tools** lists one tool,
`/providers/Microsoft.PowerApps/apis/shared_office365` ("This operation sends an email message"), and
**Can read** and **Knowledge** are empty. The registry shows the capability. It doesn't show whose
connection the tool runs on, or that the owner has left. **Details** shows Owner, Publisher and Created
by = Tom Dean, Last used = Never, and an Entra agent ID. That's where you see the departed owner, but only
because you already know Tom has left. Record it as a gap: an orphan with a live mail-send tool, and no
tile or signal flags it.

</details>

### 03: Check for the drift

The registry lists Claims Copilot. The question is whether anything in it tells you the agent runs in a
test environment.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. Search the registry for **Claims Copilot** and select it.
2. Read the list's columns and the agent's panel for anything that says which environment it's in, or
   whether it's a test or production agent.
3. Record the result.

</details>

Without the tenant: record that drift would need an environment column, and check what `agents-export.csv`
tells you that the registry wouldn't.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Reveal what the registry shows</summary>

Claims Copilot is listed, under its plain name, one entry per estate. The list's columns are **Name**,
**Status**, **Platform**, **Risks**, **Active users** and **Total**. There's no environment column, so
nothing marks an agent as test or production. A production-use candidate sitting in `Claims-Test` looks
the same as any other agent. Record it as a gap: the drift is invisible to the registry.

</details>

### 04: Write the ownership policy

Detection only helps if each finding has an owner rule and an action behind it. Write both.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. Write the owner rule in one sentence: who must own a production agent, and what happens when that
   owner leaves.
2. For Onboarding Guide and for Claims Copilot, write one action from: assign a new owner, **Block** the
   agent, or **Delete** it, and why.

</details>

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Reveal the suggested policy</summary>

- **Owner rule:** every agent in use has a present, accountable human owner; when the owner leaves, the
  agent is reassigned or blocked within a set time, and the owner's connections are reviewed.
- **Onboarding Guide:** **Block** now, then assign a new owner or delete it. Blocking stops it at once
  and can be undone; deleting can't.
- **Claims Copilot:** assign an accountable owner and move it to `Halcourt-Prod` through the release
  process, or block it. The registry lists it but can't tell you it's in a test environment, so the
  environment check has to come from your inventory.

</details>

### 05: Block Onboarding Guide and retest Scenario F

{: .important }
> Only your facilitator blocks the agent. Lab accounts can't: **Block** and **Assign new owner** are
> greyed out for you. You read the result.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

Your facilitator, with AI Administrator activated:

1. Opens the Microsoft 365 admin center → **Agents** → **All agents** → **Registry**, searches for the
   estate id and selects **Onboarding Guide**.
2. Selects **Block** and confirms. The status reads **Blocked**, and a banner reads "Agent blocked — The
   agent can no longer be installed or used in your organization."
3. After the retests, selects Onboarding Guide again → **Unblock** and confirms.

Block is the action demonstrated because it can be undone. **Delete** can't, and neither can removing the
orphaned Outlook connection: that deletes the connection, and only recreating it and signing in again as
its owner brings it back.

Then you:

1. Search the registry for Onboarding Guide and read its **Status**. Onboarding Guide has no Teams link,
   so the status is the retest: a blocked agent can't run, so it can't send mail.
2. Record what the block leaves behind: the agent, its Entra agent identity and Tom Dean's Outlook
   connection all still exist, and the owner hasn't been reassigned.

</details>

Without the tenant: reason about what blocking does to Onboarding Guide's mail-send path, and what it
leaves in place, from its rows in `agents-export.csv` and `connections-export.csv`.

### 06: Record the verdicts

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. In your [Control Efficacy Matrix]({% link worksheets/templates/04-control-efficacy-matrix.md %}), fill
   the **Agent 365** column's **F** cell.
2. Copy a **Retest log** block below the matrix: Before, After, Verdict, and Remaining risk. Say whether
   the orphan was stopped or only listed, and what the registry didn't detect: no ownerless count, no
   risk signal, no environment to show drift.
3. In the **Agent 365** column's **B**, **C**, **D** and **E** cells, note inventory value only. Read the
   **Risks** column for the agents those scenarios use. The registry exposes; it doesn't remediate.

</details>

{: .important }
> **Solves versus exposes.** Inventory and orphan detection are close to a real fix for lifecycle failures
> if, and only if, detection is wired to an action: block the agent, reassign the owner, remove the
> connection. A dashboard that lists an orphan has *exposed* the problem, which is valuable and still not
> the same as *closing* it. Record which one you actually achieved.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Reveal the suggested verdicts and reasoning</summary>

- **Agent 365 × F:** suggested **DENTS IT**. The action worked: after **Block**, Onboarding Guide's status
  read **Blocked** and it can't run, so it can't send mail. Detection didn't: **Agents without owners** read
  0, **Agents at risk** read "—", and with no environment column the Claims Copilot drift is invisible.
  The block leaves the Entra agent identity and Tom Dean's Outlook connection in place, the owner isn't
  reassigned, and **Unblock** restores everything. It becomes **CLOSES IT** only if detection is wired to
  an action you can show took effect.
- **B, C, D, E:** inventory value only, no verdict. The registry lists the agents, but the **Risks** column
  read 0 for every one.
- **The registry's blind spots:** an owner who has left isn't ownerless while the account exists, the
  **Data & tools** panel doesn't say whose connection a tool runs on, and nothing says which environment an
  agent is in.

</details>

## Summary

Governance is the control meant to find lifecycle failures. Here the registry listed the whole estate but
flagged no orphan, no risk and no drift: Onboarding Guide's departed owner showed only on its **Details**
tab, and nothing marks Claims Copilot as a test agent. The block on Onboarding Guide worked because someone
acted on your inventory. An inventory entry is exposure, and closing needs an
action. Record the verdict and remaining risk in the matrix.
