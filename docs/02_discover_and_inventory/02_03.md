# Task 02-03 — Hunt the problem estate

## Introduction

The findings aren't in any single CSV. They show up only in your inventory, where each agent's row
brings together facts from several: an owner who has left, a connection owned by someone who left, a
sandbox doing production work. This task teaches you the hunts: the specific questions that turn a
flat inventory into a risk picture. It also teaches the harder discipline: an agent that *looks*
risky is not a finding until you can show the two records that prove it, and an agent that looks scary can
be **clean** once you inspect its effective access.

## Description

You will run six targeted hunts against your inventory from Task 02-02, each looking for one problem
condition. Each hunt produces a **candidate list**, not a verdict. You then confirm every candidate with a
second data point, and — just as important — you deliberately try to **clear** the agents that only look
risky. You are building evidence, not vibes.

## Success Criteria

- [ ] You ran each of the six hunts and marked its column **yes** or **no** for every agent in your Flags table.
- [ ] For every candidate you confirmed the condition with a second data point (not just the one column that flagged it).
- [ ] You distinguished the **agent owner** from the **connection owner** wherever they differ. (Whose credential actually acts on each call is Exercise 03's question.)
- [ ] For every agent your hunts flagged, you either cleared it or confirmed the flag, with a one-line reason.
- [ ] You can explain why a sandbox or personal environment is a **candidate**, not a **verdict**.
- [ ] You compared your hunt results and clearances with the suggested ones.

## Key Tasks

### 01: Run the six hunts

Each hunt is a question about the inventory you built in Task 02-02. Copy the **Flags** table from the end
of the [Agent Estate Inventory template]({% link worksheets/templates/01-agent-estate-inventory.md %}) next
to your inventory, one row per agent: each hunt fills one column. Do
**not** assume the column that flagged an agent is the whole story — confirm with the second data point
in each hunt.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. **Orphaned: is the owner gone?** In your inventory's **Owner (status)** column, find every agent whose
   owner has left or is disabled.
   - *Confirm:* does its **Tool calls** column still list a connection? An orphan whose connection is
     still there is the dangerous kind: nothing has cut off what its owner set up.
   - *Mark* **Orphaned?** **yes** or **no** for every agent.

   <details markdown="block">
   <summary>Reveal the suggested answer</summary>

   **Onboarding Guide**: owner `tom.dean` left on 2026-01-30, and his account is still enabled. It still
   has its connection, `O365-Outlook-Onboarding`, which can send mail.

   </details>

2. **Shadow: is it built in a personal environment?** In your inventory's **Environment** column, find
   every agent in a **Developer** environment. A Developer environment belongs to one maker, and is never
   a sanctioned place to run agents for others.
   - *Confirm:* was IT ever meant to govern it? Is the environment unmanaged in your inventory?
   - *Mark* **Shadow?** **yes** or **no** for every agent.

   <details markdown="block">
   <summary>Reveal the suggested answer</summary>

   **Team Wiki** and **Meeting Notes Summarizer**, both in `Maker-Sandbox` (Developer), which is `managed`
   `No`, with Dev Kim as its only maker, and no DLP. Team Wiki is confirmed: it searches Microsoft 365 on
   Dev's own connection, so its reach is everything Dev can read. Meeting Notes Summarizer is only a
   candidate; Key Task 02 looks at it.

   </details>

3. **Test became prod: does the label lie?** In your inventory, find every agent in a **Sandbox**
   environment. Is it published, and does its **Purpose** say it does real business work, not
   experimentation?
   - *Confirm:* does the same environment show no DLP? That's production work running with sandbox
     governance.
   - *Mark* **Test→Prod?** **yes** or **no** for every agent.

   <details markdown="block">
   <summary>Reveal the suggested answer</summary>

   **Claims Copilot**, in `Claims-Test` (Sandbox). It's published and available, and it helps claims
   adjusters look up claims: real business work. `Claims-Test` has no DLP.

   </details>

4. **Shared connection: does the agent run as someone else?** For each line in your inventory's
   **Tool calls** column, flag the agent if **any one** of these is true of the connection owner:
   - they aren't the agent's owner (compare the line's owner with **Owner (status)**),
   - they're a service account, or
   - they've left or are disabled.

   With Maker-provided credentials, a service account, a flow owner or a key, the call carries that
   connection's authority, not the person chatting's, so anyone who chats with the agent acts as its
   connection owner.
   - *Confirm:* that owner's status, already in the line.
   - *Mark* **Shared connection?** **yes** or **no** for every agent.

   <details markdown="block">
   <summary>Reveal the suggested answer</summary>

   Three agents:
   - **Claims Copilot** on `DV-Claims-Shared`, owned by `svc-claims`, a service account, not the agent
     owner `priya.nair`.
   - **Sales Nudge** on `DV-CRM-Shared`, owned by `tom.dean`, not the agent owner `lena.ford`. Tom left on
     2026-01-30, and his account is still enabled.
   - **Onboarding Guide** on `O365-Outlook-Onboarding`, owned by `tom.dean`, who has left.

   </details>

5. **Unknown or ambiguous owner: is anyone personally accountable?** Flag the agent if either is true:
   1. Its **Owner (status)** is a shared mailbox, or anything that isn't a person.
   2. Its owner is active, but a **Tool calls** line belongs to someone who has left or is disabled. The
      owner can't account for authority that isn't theirs, and the person whose authority it is has gone.

   An owner who is a named person and has left isn't this flag: that's hunt 1's Orphaned. The same agent
   can take this flag and hunt 4's.
   - *Confirm:* what is the agent wired to? An ambiguously owned agent with an external integration or a
     hard-coded credential is worse than one with none.
   - *Mark* **Unknown / ambiguous owner?** **yes** or **no** for every agent.

   <details markdown="block">
   <summary>Reveal the suggested answer</summary>

   Two agents:
   - **Policy Lookup Bot**: owner `it.helpdesk` is a shared mailbox. Its HTTP request calls an external
     API with an API key in a header: a hard-coded credential.
   - **Sales Nudge**: owner `lena.ford` is active, but it runs on `DV-CRM-Shared`, owned by `tom.dean`,
     who has left. Lena can't account for Tom's CRM access, and Tom is gone.

   **Onboarding Guide** doesn't take this flag. Its owner is a known, named person who has left: that's
   hunt 1's Orphaned flag.

   </details>

6. **Nobody realizes it exists: would anyone notice it?** Using your answer to Task 02-01's Key Task 02,
   question 3, find each agent a routine review of the production environments would never reach, or whose
   owner has gone. It's a candidate: in a real estate you'd confirm it by asking the responsible team; in
   the lab, leave it as a candidate.
   - *Mark* **Nobody realizes it exists?** **yes** or **no** for every agent.

   <details markdown="block">
   <summary>Reveal the suggested answer</summary>

   **Onboarding Guide**, whose owner has left, and **Team Wiki**, in Dev Kim's own Developer environment.

   </details>

</details>

### 02: Clear the ones that only look risky

Every agent your hunts flagged is a candidate. For each one, decide: does the evidence confirm the flag,
or does the agent's effective access clear it? Clearing is real security work: you owe a reason, not a
shrug.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. List every agent your hunts flagged, with the hunt that flagged it. For each, check its effective
   access:
   - **Knowledge:** your inventory's **Grounding** column.
   - **Tools and actions:** your inventory's **Tool calls** column. Check whether any of them writes or
     sends.
   - **Acting credential:** the `auth_type` and connection owner in each **Tool calls** line.

   Without the tenant, your inventory's Grounding is Unknown: leave the agent uncleared until that check
   is done.
2. **Clear it only if its reach fits its purpose.** If what it can read and do is proportionate to its
   **Purpose**, and limited to data its users could already see, clear it, and say explicitly that its
   risk is low.
3. **Otherwise, confirm the flag.** Its effective access is the evidence.
4. **Write one line either way:** which hunt flagged it, what you checked, and why you cleared or
   confirmed it. If you clear it, change its flag to **no** in your Flags table and keep the line beside
   the table.

An agent that comes out all-"no" is unflagged, not cleared: only the agents your hunts flagged need a
line.

</details>

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Reveal the suggested clearances</summary>

Decide on your own clearances first, then compare.

- **Meeting Notes Summarizer** (hunt 2, shadow). *Checked:* no tools or actions; its **Knowledge** is one
  SharePoint source, *DevKim-OneDrive-Notes*, searched with no connection (whose access the search uses
  is Unknown until Exercise 04). *Not yet cleared.* To decide, open the source's address from the agent's
  **Knowledge** page. If you can see it and it holds only Dev's own notes, clear it as low risk, recorded
  as discovered in a personal environment. If you can't open it, record "Unknown — no access" and leave
  it uncleared.
- **Team Wiki** (hunt 2, shadow). *Checked:* its tool runs any Microsoft Graph query on Dev Kim's
  connection, and it grounds on the `HR-Benefits` and `Claims-Knowledge` sites. *Confirmed:* its reach,
  everything Dev can read plus two other teams' sites, is far beyond a personal wiki.

Every other flagged agent is confirmed by its hunt's own evidence. And no flag is not the same as
cleared: Benefits Buddy, Underwriting Assistant, Expense Helper and HR FAQ v2 (copy) take none of the six
flags, but nothing here clears them. Exercise 03 maps what they can reach.

From the pack alone you can't clear Meeting Notes Summarizer. The pack doesn't hold grounding, so it
can't be cleared until you check it.

</details>

{: .caution }
> "Sandbox equals bad" is pattern-matching, not analysis. It generates candidates; it does not close
> cases. The whole point of this hunt is to replace the vibe with evidence
> you can show someone: the two rows that prove it.

{: .note }
> A clean agent is a finding too. If you cannot justify *why* an agent is fine, it is not cleared yet — it
> is just un-reviewed.

## Summary

You ran six hunts (orphaned owner, shadow environment, test-became-prod, shared connection, ambiguous
owner, and nobody realizes it exists) and treated each result as a candidate to confirm, not a verdict. You checked each
agent's connection owner as well as its owner, and found agents running on someone else's authority:
Sales Nudge on Tom's, Claims Copilot on a service account's. Then you did the harder half: for each
flagged agent, you cleared it or confirmed the flag, with a reason. Next you total the flags and check your
work.
