# Task 02-01 — Where agents hide: discovery surfaces

## Introduction

You cannot secure what you cannot see. Before you build the inventory, you need to know **where** agents
show up, and — more importantly — where they **don't**. Every discovery surface has a viewport and a
blind spot. If you inventory from one surface only, you will confidently miss the agents that matter most:
the ones in personal and "test" environments that a routine review skips, and the ones that run on a
connection someone else owns, which the surface never shows you.

## Description

You will walk the discovery surfaces available for a Copilot Studio estate, and for each one record two
things: **what it reveals** and **what it misses**. You then name the blind spots explicitly and decide
which surface you will actually build the Halcourt Mutual inventory from. For this lab that is the live
tenant: the CSVs you filled in from it, checked against its portals. If you can't reach the tenant, work
from the filled-in **artifact pack** and read each surface's description instead of opening it.

## Success Criteria

- [ ] You can list the discovery surfaces available for a Copilot Studio estate and state, for each, what it reveals.
- [ ] For each surface you have recorded at least one thing it **misses**.
- [ ] You have named the blind spots that matter most for this estate, with the evidence for each.
- [ ] You can state the difference between an **agent owner** and a **connection owner**, and why a surface that lists only the agent owner doesn't show who owns the connections the agent uses.
- [ ] You have chosen the surface(s) you will build the inventory from, and know what you fall back to if the tenant is unavailable.

## Key Tasks

### 01: Map the discovery surfaces

Work through each surface below. For each, open it (or read its description) and write down, in your notes,
what it shows you and what it leaves out.

<!-- ![Power Platform admin center Inventory page listing Copilot Studio agents across environments](../../media/ex02_t01_kt01_s02.png) -->

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. **The agent registry.** In the [Microsoft 365 admin center](https://admin.microsoft.com){:target="_blank"},
   go to **Agents** → **All agents**.
   - Find the **Agents without owners** card. Does it count any of your agents?
   - Select one of your agents and open **Data & tools**. Does it list the knowledge and tools you found
     in Task 01-01?
2. **Power Platform admin center — Inventory.** In the
   [Power Platform admin center](https://admin.powerplatform.microsoft.com){:target="_blank"}, go to
   **Manage** → **Inventory** and filter to agents.
   - Which environments does your filter cover?
   - What does **Owner** show for an agent whose connection belongs to someone else, such as Sales Nudge?
3. **The CoE Starter Kit.** Read only: it isn't installed in the lab tenant. The Center of Excellence
   Starter Kit syncs an inventory of makers, apps, flows and agents into Dataverse and shows it in
   dashboards. Think about what a scheduled sync could miss.
4. **Agent identities.** You used **Entra ID** → **Agents** → **Agent identities** in Task 01-02. What
   does an identity tell you about where its agent lives, or what it can reach?
5. **Your five CSVs.** What do they show that no single portal shows on one screen? What don't they hold?

</details>

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Reveal the suggested reveals and misses</summary>

Write your own notes first, then compare.

- **The agent registry.**
  - *Reveals:* a tenant-wide catalog of agents, with summary cards and, per agent, its description,
    publish status, last update, and **Data & tools**.
  - *Misses:* **Agents without owners** counts only agents whose owner account was deleted, so an owner
    who has left, account enabled or disabled, doesn't appear there. **Data & tools** can be empty, so
    check an agent's knowledge and tools in Copilot Studio.
- **Power Platform admin center — Inventory.**
  - *Reveals:* the agents in the environments it enumerates, with **Owner**, **Created on**,
    **Environment type** and **Connectors**, and a CSV download.
  - *Misses:* only what you can see and filter to; it's easy to review `Halcourt-Prod` and never look at
    `Maker-Sandbox` or `Halcourt (default)`. **Owner** is the agent's owner, not the connections' owners,
    and **Modified on** shows a dash for agents.
- **The CoE Starter Kit.**
  - *Reveals:* the closest thing to a single pane, across makers, apps, flows and agents.
  - *Misses:* anything its sync doesn't reach or isn't configured for, such as environments excluded from
    the sync or added since its last run. It reports the agent owner, not whose credential a connection
    carries.
- **Agent identities.**
  - *Reveals:* each agent's Entra identity, which outlives a departed owner.
  - *Misses:* the environment, the grounding and the connections; you have to match each identity back to
    an agent, through its sponsor. An identity with no matching agent is something to investigate, not a
    deleted agent's leftover: deleting an agent deletes its identity too.
- **Your five CSVs.**
  - *Reveals:* everything lined up in one place: owner, connection owner, `auth_type`, identity and
    environment.
  - *Misses:* grounding, which has no column, and anything that changed since you gathered them.

</details>

### 02: Name the blind spots

A surface tour is only useful if you can say where each one goes dark. Record these blind spots against
the Halcourt Mutual estate specifically.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. **Check which environments a routine review covers.** Look at each environment's `type` and
   `maker_access` in your `environments-export.csv`. Which would a "review the production environments"
   pass skip, and why?

   Access is part of this blind spot. Anything that reads agent records environment by environment — a
   script, `pac`, a Dataverse query — sees only the environments where your account holds a security role.
   A Developer environment's only System Administrator is the maker who owns it. An empty result can mean
   *no access*, not *no agents*.
2. **Two you've already met.** In Task 01-01 you found that an agent's owner isn't always its
   connection's owner (step 7), and that HTTP requests and flows aren't connections at all (step 6). Note
   both as blind spots of any surface that lists only agent owners, or only connections. Task 02-03 hunts
   the owner mismatch.
3. **Some agents are in nobody's mental inventory.** An agent can be published and active while the person
   who would review it is gone, or while it sits in an environment no one governs. No surface flags "nobody
   realizes this exists" for you — it's a candidate you corroborate, not something a lookup proves.
4. **Decide your working inventory.** For this lab, build it from your CSVs — the evidence you gathered
   from the tenant; note when you gathered it — and corroborate material facts against the live surfaces
   (PPAC, Copilot Studio, Entra, the agent registry), which also show each agent's grounding. If you
   can't reach the tenant, build it from the filled-in artifact pack, which covers every step offline.

</details>

{: .note }
> The point of a surface inventory is not to pick the "best" tool. It is to understand that each tool has a
> blind spot, so you can deliberately cover the gaps. Your five CSVs, lined up, exist precisely
> because no single live view puts owner, connection owner, identity, and environment in one place.

## Summary

You mapped the discovery surfaces for a Copilot Studio estate — the Microsoft 365 admin center agent
registry, PPAC, the CoE Starter Kit, Microsoft Graph for agent identities, and your five CSVs — and recorded
what each one reveals and misses. You named the blind spots that will bite hardest at Halcourt Mutual,
with the evidence for each. With your working inventory chosen, you are ready to combine the CSVs into a
single inventory.
