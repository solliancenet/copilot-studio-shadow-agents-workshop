# Task 00-02 — Meet Halcourt Mutual

## Introduction

Before you hunt for problems, learn the terrain. A security review that starts by naming the environments,
the identities, and the systems in play is far harder to fool than one that dives straight into a single
agent. This task orients you to Halcourt Mutual, its four Power Platform environments, and where the day
is headed.

## Description

You will read the **four environments** in the Power Platform admin center and record them in your blank
`environments-export.csv` (or read them from the filled-in export if you can't reach the tenant), walk the
day's arc from **DISCOVER** to **OPERATIONALIZE**, and look over all five CSVs to feel how hard the three
questions really are. You record facts here, not findings: whether any of them is a problem is a call
you make in Exercise 02. For now you are building a mental map so the later exercises land. You will also
locate the Estate Binder templates you fill in as you go.

## Success Criteria

- [ ] You can name Halcourt Mutual's four environments and, for each, say its type, DLP policy, and managed status.
- [ ] *(Live tenant only)* Your `environments-export.csv` has a row for each of the four environments.
- [ ] You can state the day's arc: **DISCOVER → MAP → BREAK → CONTROL → RETEST → OPERATIONALIZE**, and which exercise each phase maps to.
- [ ] You have looked over all five CSVs and can restate the three questions — how many agents, who owns them, what can they access — in your own words.
- [ ] You know where the Estate Binder templates live and that you build one deliverable per phase.

## Key Tasks

### 01: Read the four environments

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. In the **Power Platform admin center** at `https://admin.powerplatform.microsoft.com`, choose **Manage**, then **Environments**. Find your estate's four environments: the three that carry your estate id, and `Halcourt (default)`, which every estate shares. The list can also include other estates' environments and any others in the tenant; leave them out. It
   also shows **your own lab environment** (`Lab-<estate_id>-NN`): it isn't one of the estate's four, so
   don't record it, but note that it's there. Exercise 03 sends you into it. Each environment shows its **type**. To see whether one is **managed**, open its **…** menu: it offers **Edit Managed Environments** when the environment is managed, and **Enable Managed Environments** when it isn't.
2. In the left rail, choose **Security** → **Data and privacy** → **Data policy**. The **Applied to** column shows which environments each **DLP policy** covers.
3. For **maker access**, find out who can build agents in each environment. Open the environment and go to **Settings** → **Users + permissions** → **Security roles**, select **Environment Maker**, then **…** → **Members**. Halcourt grants this role person by person, not through a group, so the list names people one by one, mixed in with accounts that aren't Halcourt's.
   - **Go by the Username column.** Halcourt's people have your estate id just before the `@` (`<name>.<estate_id>@<tenant domain>`). Every other account is Microsoft's own (`CDSUserManagement@onmicrosoft.com`, `flowdev@microsoft.com`, and so on) or your facilitator's administrator, who built the lab. Leave those out, and your own account too if it appears.
   - **Ignore the `#`** in front of some names. The admin center adds it to Microsoft's accounts and to some people's alike, so it doesn't tell you which is which.

   Two environments work differently:
   - **Maker-Sandbox** is a Developer environment: its owner builds as **System Administrator**, so read that role's **Members** instead, and record the owner as `<name> (owner)`. Environment Maker there lists only Microsoft accounts.
   - **Halcourt (default)**: every licensed user can build there without being listed, so record `All users`.
4. Record the four environments in your blank [`environments-export.csv`]({% link worksheets/artifact-pack/blank/environments-export.csv %}), one row each: `environment`, `type`, `dlp_policy` (empty when no policy applies), `managed` (`Yes` or `No`), and `maker_access`. For `maker_access`, write the makers' names in alphabetical order, separated by semicolons, without your estate id. Your rows should match this table, with your estate id on the names of your environments and DLP policy:

   | Environment | Type | DLP policy | Managed | Maker access |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | Halcourt (default) | Default | *(none)* | No | All users |
   | Halcourt-Prod | Production | Halcourt-Prod-DLP | Yes | IT Helpdesk; Lena Ford; Marcus Webb; Priya Nair |
   | Claims-Test | Sandbox | *(none)* | No | Priya Nair |
   | Maker-Sandbox | Developer | *(none)* | No | Dev Kim (owner) |

   Without the tenant: read the same facts from the filled-in [`environments-export.csv`]({% link worksheets/artifact-pack/environments-export.csv %}).

5. Read the table without judging it yet. Just notice the shape: which environments carry a DLP policy and which do not; which are managed; who is allowed to build in each. You will come back to these facts in Exercise 02 and decide what they mean.
6. Note the trust-boundary question you will keep asking all day: **whose authority does an agent exercise, and what can it reach when it does?** Where an agent lives — and whether that environment has guardrails — is the first clue.

</details>

### 02: Walk the day arc and look over your CSVs

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. Fix the through-line in your head. The whole day moves along one arc:

   ```mermaid
   flowchart LR
     D[DISCOVER] --> M[MAP] --> B[BREAK] --> C[CONTROL] --> R[RETEST] --> O[OPERATIONALIZE]
   ```

2. Map each phase to where you will do it:
   - **DISCOVER** — inventory every agent, everywhere ([Exercise 02]({% link docs/02_discover_and_inventory/02_discover_and_inventory.md %})).
   - **MAP** — trace identity, authorization, and effective access ([Exercise 03]({% link docs/03_identity_and_effective_access/03_identity_and_effective_access.md %})).
   - **BREAK** — observe the failure modes in controlled scenarios ([Exercise 04]({% link docs/04_break_the_agents/04_break_the_agents.md %})).
   - **CONTROL / RETEST** — put Microsoft's controls to the test and rule **CLOSES IT / DENTS IT / MISSES IT** ([Exercise 05]({% link docs/05_test_the_controls/05_test_the_controls.md %})).
   - **OPERATIONALIZE** — turn it into a repeatable baseline ([Exercise 06]({% link docs/06_security_baseline/06_security_baseline.md %})).
3. Give the five CSVs two minutes total. If you're working in the tenant, they are still header rows, apart from the environments you just recorded: read the columns, because you fill them in during Exercise 01. Without the tenant, skim the filled-in set. Either way, do not analyze — just feel the weight of the three questions: how many agents are there, who owns each one, and what does each one connect to? Notice that no single file answers any question on its own; the answers live in the joins between them.
4. Open the [Estate Binder]({% link worksheets/index.md %}). You will produce one deliverable per phase — the first is the **Agent Estate Inventory** in Exercise 02. Keep the binder handy; you fill it in as you work.

</details>

{: .tip }
> No single file tells the whole story. An agent whose owner has left, a connection owned by someone who
> is gone, an agent in an environment with no DLP — those only appear when you **join** the files. That is
> the skill Exercise 02 builds; today just notice how the pieces fit together.

## Summary

You have met Halcourt Mutual: a regulated insurer with four Power Platform environments, a fleet of
maker-built Copilot Studio agents, and no one who can answer the three questions. You read each
environment's type, DLP policy, managed status, and maker access, walked the day's arc from DISCOVER to
OPERATIONALIZE, and found the Estate Binder you will fill in along the way. With the terrain mapped, you
are ready to start counting agents in Exercise 02.
