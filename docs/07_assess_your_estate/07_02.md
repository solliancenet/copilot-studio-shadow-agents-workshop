# Task 07-02 — Your top 3 first-week actions

## Introduction

Seven answers are a diagnosis, not a plan. This task turns them into three things you can
actually do in your first week back — each with an owner and a date — plus one sentence
that gets leadership to back them. A finding nobody owns and nobody funds changes nothing.

## Description

From your seven answers, choose the three most important actions. Record each in the action table of the
[Own-Estate Assessment]({% link worksheets/templates/06-own-estate-assessment.md %}) worksheet: the action,
the question it addresses, the owner, the due date, and what "done" looks like.

Rank candidates by **blast radius** first. Blast radius is how much sensitive data, or how many write and
approve actions, an agent can reach. Anything severe that you can contain right away goes to the top — for
example, disabling a connection or unsharing an agent. Effort only breaks ties between actions of similar
importance. Then write one sentence for leadership about why this matters now, and name the very first
thing you will look at in your tenant.

## Success Criteria

- [ ] Exactly three first-week actions are written, ranked by blast radius, with any severe contain-now action at the top.
- [ ] Each action records the question it addresses from Task 07-01, a named owner, a due date, and a "done when".
- [ ] At least one action is a zero-budget quick win — a read, inventory, or scope change you can do yourself this week.
- [ ] One leadership sentence is drafted that names the asset at risk *and* the moment.
- [ ] You can state, out loud, the first thing you will look at in your tenant in your first week back.

## Key Tasks

### 01: Choose your top three first-week actions

Prioritize ruthlessly. You are picking three, not thirty — the point is momentum, and a
short list you actually finish beats a long list you admire.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. **List candidates.** Write one candidate action per gap you found in Task 07-01 — the
   raw material is already on the page.
2. **Rank by blast radius.** Blast radius is how much sensitive data, or how many write
   and approve actions, the agent can reach. Rank the list by that first. Then move
   anything severe that you can contain right away to the top — for example, disabling a
   connection or unsharing an agent. Use effort only to break ties between actions of
   similar importance.
3. **Pick three, and bias toward a quick win.** Make at least one a zero-budget action you
   can start immediately — for example: pull the five estate exports for your tenant; list
   the agents whose owner account is disabled; find the connections owned by departed users
   or running as service accounts; check which environments have no DLP applied; match
   your active agents against the agent identities in Entra and investigate any that don't.
   These are the same joins you ran against Halcourt, now pointed at home.
4. **Finish each row.** For each action, fill the worksheet's action table: the action,
   the question it addresses, the owner, the due date, and **Done when** — what you will
   see once it's finished. An action without an owner, a date, and a finish line is a
   wish, not a plan.

</details>

### 02: Draft the one-sentence case to leadership

The three actions are for you and your team. This sentence is for the person who controls
your time and budget — it has to land in one breath.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. **Name the asset in business terms.** Say "customer PII," "executive compensation," or
   "the ability to approve payments" — not "a Dataverse connector." Leadership funds
   protection of *things they already care about*.
2. **Name the moment.** Every agent created since May 2026 has an Entra Agent ID. Older
   agents may still use a legacy app registration until Microsoft migrates them. Agent 365
   went GA on May 1, 2026. The ability to build agents outpaced the governance around them.
   That gap is why this is urgent now, not next quarter.
3. **Make the ask concrete and cheap.** You are asking for time to inventory and scope,
   not for a platform purchase. A small, specific ask is far easier to approve than a
   vague call for "more security."
4. **Compress to one sentence.** Draft it, cut it, and read it aloud. If a busy executive
   would remember it after one hearing, it is done. Write the final sentence into the
   worksheet.

</details>

{: .tip }
> A case that works sounds like this shape: *"Anyone in the company can now build an agent
> that quietly acts with someone else's access to [our most sensitive data], and today we
> can't say how many exist or what they can reach — I need [two weeks / one analyst] to
> inventory and scope them before that becomes an incident."* Make it true for your tenant,
> then make it shorter.

## Summary

You now know what to look at in your own tenant in your first week back, and how to make
the case for fixing it. You leave with all three: a **diagnosis** (your seven answers), a **plan**
(three actions, each with an owner, a due date, and a finish line), and a **sentence** that funds it. Carry
these into the [wrap-up and hand-off]({% link docs/08_wrap_up_and_handoff/08_wrap_up_and_handoff.md %})
— the resources there help you keep the work going after today.
