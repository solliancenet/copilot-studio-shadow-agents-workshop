# Task 07-02 — Your top 3 Monday actions

## Introduction

Seven answers are a diagnosis, not a plan. This task turns them into three things you can
actually do Monday morning — each with an owner and a date — plus one sentence that gets
leadership to back them. A finding nobody owns and nobody funds changes nothing.

## Description

From your seven answers, choose the three highest-leverage actions and record each with an
owner and a *by-when* in the [Own-Estate Assessment]({% link worksheets/templates/06-own-estate-assessment.md %})
worksheet. Rank candidates by **blast radius × ease**: blast radius is how much sensitive
data or how many write and approve actions an agent can reach; ease is how quickly you can
act without a budget request. Then write one sentence for leadership about why this matters
now, and name the very first thing you will look at in your tenant.

## Success Criteria

- [ ] Exactly three Monday actions are written, each with a named owner and a date.
- [ ] Each action ties back to one of the seven questions from Task 07-01.
- [ ] At least one action is a zero-budget quick win — a read, inventory, or scope change you can do yourself this week.
- [ ] One leadership sentence is drafted that names the asset at risk *and* the moment.
- [ ] You can state, out loud, the first thing you will look at in your tenant on Monday.

## Key Tasks

### 01: Choose your top three Monday-morning actions

Prioritize ruthlessly. You are picking three, not thirty — the point is momentum, and a
short list you actually finish beats a long list you admire.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. **List candidates.** Write one candidate action per gap you found in Task 07-01 — the
   raw material is already on the page.
2. **Score by blast radius × ease.** Blast radius is how much sensitive data, or how many
   write and approve actions, the agent can reach. Ease is whether you can do it yourself
   this week — a read, inventory, or scope change — versus something that needs budget or
   a project. Rank the list.
3. **Pick three, and bias toward a quick win.** Make at least one a zero-budget action you
   can start immediately — for example: pull the five estate exports for your tenant; list
   the agents whose owner account is disabled; find the connections owned by departed users
   or running as service accounts; check which environments have no DLP applied. These are
   the same joins you ran against Halcourt, now pointed at home.
4. **Assign an owner and a date to each.** An action without a named owner and a *by-when*
   is a wish, not a plan. Write both into the worksheet.

</details>

### 02: Draft the one-sentence case to leadership

The three actions are for you and your team. This sentence is for the person who controls
your time and budget — it has to land in one breath.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. **Name the asset in business terms.** Say "customer PII," "executive compensation," or
   "the ability to approve payments" — not "a Dataverse connector." Leadership funds
   protection of *things they already care about*.
2. **Name the moment.** Since March 2026 every new Copilot Studio agent gets an Entra
   identity automatically, and Agent 365 went GA in May 2026. The ability to build agents
   outpaced the governance around them — that gap is the reason this is urgent now, not
   next quarter.
3. **Make the ask concrete and cheap.** You are asking for time to inventory and scope,
   not for a platform purchase. A small, specific ask is far easier to approve than a
   vague call for "more security."
4. **Compress to one sentence.** Draft it, cut it, and read it aloud. If a busy executive
   would remember it after one hearing, it is done. Write the final sentence into the
   worksheet.

</details>

{: .tip }
> A case that works sounds like this shape: *"Anyone in the company can now build an agent
> that quietly acts with someone else's access to [our most sensitive data], and today we
> can't say how many exist or what they can reach — I need [two weeks / one analyst] to
> inventory and scope them before that becomes an incident."* Make it true for your tenant,
> then make it shorter.

## Summary

You now know what to look at in your own tenant on Monday, and how to make the case for
fixing it. You leave with all three: a **diagnosis** (your seven answers), a **plan**
(three actions, each with an owner and a date), and a **sentence** that funds it. Carry
these into the [wrap-up and hand-off]({% link docs/08_wrap_up_and_handoff/08_wrap_up_and_handoff.md %})
— the resources there help you keep the work going after today.
