# Task 07-01 — The seven questions

## Introduction

The whole day pointed here. You learned to discover, map, break, and control an estate
that was not yours; the seven questions are the reusable frame you take home. Answer them
for your own tenant and you have the start of a real agent security program. You will not
finish an inventory in the time you have — the win is knowing exactly where each answer
lives and which ones you cannot answer yet.

## Description

Working the [Own-Estate Assessment]({% link worksheets/templates/06-own-estate-assessment.md %})
worksheet, write an answer — or an explicit *"we don't know yet, and here is how we'll
find out"* — for each of the seven questions. For every one, reuse the lab technique you
already practiced and point it at the equivalent Microsoft surface in your own tenant. The
table below is your crosswalk from what you did against Halcourt to what you do at home.

| Question | What you practiced in the lab | Where to look in your tenant |
|---|---|---|
| **Discovery** — how will we inventory our agents? | Joined the five estate exports to list every agent across *all* environments, including the personal ones | Power Platform admin center (Agents view), CoE Starter Kit agent inventory, the Agent 365 admin surface, and Microsoft Graph for workload identities — no single source is complete |
| **Ownership** — who is responsible? | Separated the *agent* owner from the *connection* owner; flagged shared mailboxes and service accounts | Agent owner in PPAC / Copilot Studio; connection owner under Connections; cross-check every owner against Entra account status |
| **Identity** — which identities need review? | Enumerated the auto-created agent (workload) identities and the accounts connections actually run as | Entra enterprise apps / service principals for the agent identities; the service accounts and shared mailboxes behind connections |
| **Access** — which systems/data are highest risk? | Mapped *effective* access: Dataverse rows and PII, whole-site SharePoint grounding, HTTP and MCP endpoints | Dataverse security roles and row-level security; the SharePoint scope each agent grounds on; custom connectors, HTTP actions, and MCP servers, and how each authenticates |
| **Controls** — what do we already have? | Tested DLP, environment strategy, Purview labels, Conditional Access, and logging against real failures | Power Platform DLP policies (and which environments they cover); your environment strategy; Purview labels and DLP; Conditional Access, including workload identities |
| **Detection** — would we know if something went wrong? | Asked whether you could reconstruct *who really asked* when a shared identity acts | Purview audit and Power Platform activity logging; Defender for Cloud Apps; and the attribution gap that opens when a shared or service identity is the actor |
| **Gaps** — what do we investigate first? | Prioritized findings by effective access and blast radius, not by how risky an agent *sounded* | Your own ranked list — start where the reachable data is most sensitive and the fix is cheapest |

{: .important }
> The findings never live in a single file. In the lab they lived in the *joins* — an
> agent whose owner is disabled, a connection owned by someone who left, an environment
> with no DLP. Your tenant is the same: pull the pieces, then look at what only shows up
> when you put them side by side.

## Success Criteria

- [ ] All seven questions have a written answer, or an explicit "unknown — and here is how we'll find out."
- [ ] Discovery names the specific surfaces you will inventory from and the five exports you will pull.
- [ ] Ownership distinguishes the agent owner from the connection owner, and flags any shared-mailbox, service-account, or departed-owner cases you already suspect.
- [ ] Access ranks at least your top two highest-risk data reaches.
- [ ] Detection states honestly whether you could reconstruct who really asked when a shared identity acts.
- [ ] Gaps names the single question you will investigate first, and why.

## Key Tasks

### 01: Answer "what do we have?" — Discovery, Ownership, Identity, Access

The first four questions build your picture of the estate: what exists, who is
accountable, which identities act, and what those identities can reach.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. **Discovery.** Decide where you will pull your inventory from — the Power Platform
   admin center "Agents" view, the CoE Starter Kit agent inventory, the Agent 365 admin
   surface, and Microsoft Graph for the auto-created workload identities. Note that no
   single source is complete; you are building the join, exactly as you did with the five
   Halcourt exports. Write those sources into the worksheet. *(Offline path: if you cannot
   export yet, use the five artifact-pack CSVs — agents, connections, agent-identities,
   users, environments — as the target schema for what to pull.)*
2. **Ownership.** For each source, capture *both* the agent owner and the connection
   owner. In the lab the connection owner — not the agent owner — was frequently the real
   actor. Write down any owners you already suspect are shared mailboxes, service
   accounts, or people who have left the organization.
3. **Identity.** List which identities you would review first: the agent workload
   identities created automatically since March 2026, any service accounts your
   connections run as, and any shared or departed accounts still holding live
   connections. Confirm in your tenant how each of these appears in Entra — the exact
   listing is a 2026 detail worth verifying rather than assuming.
4. **Access.** For your two or three most-used agents, write what they can *actually*
   reach — which Dataverse tables (and whether row-level security is switched on), the
   SharePoint scope they ground on, and any HTTP or MCP endpoints and how those
   authenticate. Rank them by the sensitivity of the data behind them, not by the agent's
   name.

</details>

### 02: Answer "are we covered?" — Controls, Detection, Gaps

The last three questions turn the picture into a judgment: what protects the estate today,
whether you would notice a failure, and where you start.

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. **Controls.** Inventory what you already run — DLP policies and which environments they
   cover, your environment strategy, Purview labels and DLP, and Conditional Access. Mark
   honestly which agents and environments each control actually touches. A DLP policy
   applied to production only still leaves your default and personal environments wide
   open, and that is usually where the shadow agents live.
2. **Detection.** Ask the day's hardest detection question: if a shared or service
   identity acts on a user's behalf, can your logs tell you *who really asked?* Write down
   where your agent activity is logged today — Purview audit, Power Platform activity
   logging, Defender for Cloud Apps — and mark where the attribution gap is. Treat the
   exact agent-action coverage as something to verify in your tenant; the 2026 signal is
   still settling.
3. **Gaps.** From the six answers above, name the single question you will investigate
   first. Prioritize by blast radius — what the agent can reach — and by how cheaply you
   can close it. This becomes the first of your Monday actions in the next task.

</details>

{: .note }
> Where an answer depends on a fast-moving 2026 product detail — the exact Agent 365 admin
> views, whether Conditional Access reaches your agent identities, how completely Purview
> and Defender see Copilot Studio activity — write it down as *"verify in tenant"* rather
> than assuming. Naming the uncertainty is part of the assessment.

## Summary

You now have a diagnosis of your own estate: seven answers, or seven honest "we don't know
yet, here's how" entries. The frame is reusable — every time a new agent, connection, or
environment appears, you run it back through these seven questions. In the next task you
turn the diagnosis into a plan.
