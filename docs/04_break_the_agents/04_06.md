# Task 04-06 — Lifecycle

## Introduction

Permissions and connections outlive the assumptions that created them. Someone builds an agent "just for
testing" and it quietly starts doing production work. An owner leaves the company, HR records the
departure, and nobody runs the leaver process: the account stays enabled, the agent they built keeps
running, and the connections they created keep working. The survivor can still act, long after anyone is
accountable for it. Lifecycle failures are the
least dramatic and among the most durable.

## Description

You will examine two lifecycle candidates. **Onboarding Guide** is owned by **Tom Dean**: you will pull together, from your inventory, whether he
is still with the company, his account's status, whether the agent is still published, and whether the
Office 365 Outlook (**send mail**) connection Tom created still works. **Claims Copilot** lives in the `Claims-Test` sandbox: you will check
whether there is evidence it is doing production work, and what it carries with it if so.

You will **observe and record** whether each survivor can still act. Do **not** actually send mail or alter
production data; confirming the capability exists is the finding.

## Success Criteria

- [ ] You recorded Onboarding Guide's owner, his account status, and that the agent is still published.
- [ ] You recorded that it can still send mail as its departed owner.

## Key Tasks

### 01: Trace ownership and status

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Expand this section for detailed steps</summary>

1. **Onboarding Guide.** From your inventory and Flags table (Exercise 02), copy into your Scenario F notes:
   - its owner and status: `tom.dean`, left 2026-01-30, account still enabled;
   - that it's published;
   - its **Calls** line: `O365-Outlook-Onboarding`, which sends mail as Tom;
   - its flags: Orphaned and Shared connection.
2. If an active, published agent is owned by someone who has left, write the contradiction plainly. That is
   an orphan. If the leaver's account is still enabled, nothing has cut off his authority either.

   > A departure HR has recorded doesn't retire anything. Until someone disables the account, the leaver's
   > connections keep working; disabling it stops the ones signed in as him at their next token refresh. Even
   > then the agent and its Entra identity are separate objects with their own lifecycle: they stay published and
   > in place until someone deletes or reassigns them.
   {: .important }

3. **Claims Copilot.** From the same places, copy into your Scenario F notes:
   - its environment: `Claims-Test` (Sandbox · unmanaged · no DLP);
   - that it's published, and what its **Purpose** says it does;
   - its **Calls** line: `DV-Claims-Shared`, as `svc-claims`;
   - its flags: Test→Prod and Shared connection.

   Published, doing real work, and in a sandbox with no data policy: those make Claims Copilot a
   **production-use candidate**, not proof. Two things would confirm it: its usage in Copilot Studio
   **Analytics** (real conversations, from people outside the test team), or real customers' claims in the
   records it reads. Your lab account can see neither, so record it as a candidate and name those two as the
   missing evidence.

</details>

<details markdown="block">
<summary>Reveal the suggested Scenario F row</summary>

- **Attack:** exploitation of a lifecycle gap — an orphaned agent, or a test agent doing production work.
- **Asset:** the mail-send capability, through a leaver's connection (Onboarding Guide); a sandbox with no
  data policy and no review, carrying a broad acting credential (Claims Copilot).
- **Trust boundary crossed:** the assumption "an owner exists and reviews this" no longer holds; the
  "test" label no longer matches real exposure.
- **Impact:** an unowned agent with live mail-send persists indefinitely; a "test" agent carries broad
  production access with no owner review.
- **Existing control:** record what exists today (any CoE / lifecycle policy? environment separation? or
  nothing?) and whether it caught these survivors.
- **Why the owner's departure changed nothing:** nobody ran the leaver process. Tom's departure is
  recorded, but his account is still enabled, so his connections still work. Disabling him would stop the
  connections at their next token refresh — but not the agent or its Entra identity, which follow the
  agent, not the owner. And no promotion gate re-reviewed Claims Copilot when its "test" environment
  became real.
- **Likely fixes to test in Exercise 05:** joiner-mover-leaver hygiene, and orphan detection through the
  Agent 365 registry (plus the CoE Starter Kit).

</details>

## Summary

You examined two lifecycle candidates — an agent whose owner left without anyone disabling his account,
and a "test" agent that may be doing production work — and showed, from your own inventory, whether each can still act. Neither
failure needs an attack, just time and the absence of a lifecycle process. The evidence sits in more than one place: the agent, its owner's account,
its connection, and its environment. Carry this evidence into Exercise 05 and test which controls prevent,
reduce, detect, or leave it unchanged.
